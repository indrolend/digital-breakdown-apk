# Traversal lab playtest

Double-click `Playtest Traversal Lab.cmd` at the repository root. It builds the
latest committed or working source incrementally, then opens Data directly in
the traversal lab.

The center lane contains level gaps of 1.5, 2.0, and 2.5 world units. The right
lane tests ascending jumps. The left lane tests ledge catches, mantles, and
jump-to-lunge movement. Misses land on the ordinary floor, and every platform
is low enough to approach again without restarting.

Test each lane first without sprinting, then with sprinting. Do not try to prove
that the hardest move is possible. The useful question is whether an intended
move is readable, repeatable, and satisfying.

For each section, note:

- understood immediately / understood after experimenting / unclear;
- comfortable / engaged / demanding / unpleasant;
- approximate attempts before the first success;
- whether later successes became consistent;
- whether the camera helped or fought the movement;
- whether a miss felt deserved;
- whether ledge recovery felt intentional or accidental;
- whether the platform looked easier or harder than it actually was.

Also note any maneuver that feels especially good even if it is not the route
you expected. Those discoveries should inform optional routes and shortcuts.

The lab deliberately contains no enemies, objectives, void, or resource
pressure. Once the isolated movement bands feel right, a later playtest should
combine only one calibrated traversal demand with combat at a time.
